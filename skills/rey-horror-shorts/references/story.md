# Horror story engine

The hook promises an impossible rule. The ending proves that the characters can understand and overcome the threat through evidence and action.

## 80–90 second beat map

| Time | Story job | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5s | State the rule and consequence | Immediate behavior or image proving the town takes it seriously |
| 5–15s | Show the adapted world | Infrastructure, ritual or daily behavior shaped by the rule |
| 15–28s | Introduce protagonist and need | A concrete reason to risk or challenge the rule |
| 28–40s | Breach the rule | A visible action and immediate first consequence |
| 40–55s | Escalate the threat | Two consistent examples of what feeds, attracts, weakens or limits it |
| 55–65s | Discover and test | The protagonist forms a hypothesis and performs a small risky test |
| 65–80s | Execute the plan | The clue becomes the mechanism of the confrontation |
| 80–87s | Resolve the threat | Destruction, deprivation or stable containment is visibly completed |
| 87–90s | Show aftermath | The town changes its system or behavior because the problem is solved |

Target roughly 210–240 spoken words. Leave space for silence, reactions and the monster reveal.

## Resolution contract

Before approving a script, be able to answer:

1. What exact rule governs the threat?
2. Which two earlier moments demonstrate that rule?
3. What does the protagonist infer?
4. How do they test the inference before risking the full plan?
5. What action defeats or stably contains the threat?
6. What prevents the same problem from immediately returning?
7. What lasting change proves the conflict is resolved?

Reject endings based on an unintroduced weakness, sudden rescue, unexplained magic, “it was all a dream,” an unrelated joke, or the monster simply leaving. A bittersweet cost is allowed when it follows from the established mechanism.

## Tone

Narrate impossible events with documentary sincerity. The premise can create dark irony, but the narrator does not wink at it. Prefer specific physical consequences over vague claims that something is “terrifying.”
