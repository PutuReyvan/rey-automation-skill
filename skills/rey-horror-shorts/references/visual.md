# Horror visual direction

Choose a visual mode according to the story rather than changing styles mid-episode.

## Original blocky toon 3D

Use readable toy-game proportions, simple materials and strong silhouettes. Keep characters original; “Roblox-like” refers only to broad blocky staging, not copied avatars or assets. Use the sibling `inkbeat-shorts` system for rigging, acting, camera and render stability.

- Give the protagonist a consistent silhouette, palette and prop.
- Animate decisions through head focus, anticipation, hand contact, weight shifts and purposeful movement. Mouth motion alone is not acting.
- Make the threat visually obey its rule: light breaks it, sound attracts it, water limits it, or another established mechanism visibly affects its body.

## Restrained stylized 2D

Use controlled shapes, limited colors, negative space and clear depth layers. Keep parallax and camera movement purposeful. Do not replace causal action with abstract texture or random symbols.

## Shot variety and continuity

For a 90-second short, plan 10–14 distinct setups across establishing geography, daily adaptation, protagonist action, warning insert, partial reveal, threat point of view, test, plan, confrontation and aftermath. A color change or closer crop alone is not a new setup.

Keep captions around 70% of frame height, above platform UI and away from faces or key clues. Test the monster silhouette, protagonist action and resolution mechanism at phone size.
