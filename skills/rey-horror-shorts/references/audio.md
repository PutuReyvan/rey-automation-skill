# Narration, TTS and sound

Use a calm, sincere documentary delivery. Lower and slow the voice only when the threat becomes physically present; build pace during the plan; settle during the aftermath. Do not perform the premise as a joke.

## Two different script deliverables

**Actor brief:** may include title, length, direction, delivery notes, speaker labels, pronunciation, pause definitions and alternate-take instructions.

**TTS-ready text:** contains only spoken words plus sparse voice-delivery tags supported by the selected provider/model. Remove headings, word counts, recording specifications, separators, speaker metadata, bracketed production notes and inline alternatives. Put alternate takes in their own block. Do not ask the voice model to create exact leader silence; add precise silence during editing.

Preserve approved wording when converting between formats. If multiple character voices are required, generate or record separate clean stems so timing and mixing remain controllable.

## Sound design

- Begin with recognizable town ambience, then remove or distort one layer when the rule is breached.
- Give the threat one recurring sound motif tied to its mechanism.
- Use low music and sparse effects beneath narration. Do not place a hit on every cut.
- Align effects to visible causes and preserve clean handles for editing.
- Listen to the complete mix. Metadata and waveform checks cannot judge dread, intelligibility or masking.
