# Suspense and reveal

Fear comes from understanding enough of the rule to anticipate what will happen, then watching the protagonist risk the consequence.

## Reveal ladder

1. Show the town's precaution before explaining all of it.
2. Show an environmental reaction: failed light, moving water, displaced objects, distorted sound or an impossible shadow.
3. Show a partial silhouette that establishes direction and scale.
4. Show one consistent interaction with the world.
5. Reveal the full threat only when the protagonist must act against it.

Do not hide every scene in darkness. Preserve readable silhouettes, geography, facial reactions and the mechanism on a phone screen. Camera shake, glitches, red flashes and loud hits are accents, not substitutes for staging.

## Escalation

- Each scare changes the state: distance closes, protection fails, the threat grows, a route disappears or the cost increases.
- Reuse a visual or sound motif so the audience learns to recognize danger.
- Let a clue serve two jobs: it frightens the audience during escalation and later enables the solution.
- Hold the decisive state long enough to understand how the plan succeeds.

The final confrontation should be the clearest presentation of the threat's rule, not the most visually chaotic scene.
