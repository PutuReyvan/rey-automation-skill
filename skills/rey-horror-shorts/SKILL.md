# Rey Horror Shorts v0.1

Create concise horror that treats an impossible premise as real local history. Speak Indonesian to Rey; write the story and production deliverables in the requested language. Keep the horror original and platform-appropriate unless the user explicitly requests a different allowed audience level.

## Workflow

1. **Define the rule:** identify the town rule, the observable consequence of breaking it, the protagonist's need and the threat's operating mechanism. The premise may be strange; its cause and effect may not be random.
2. **Design the resolution first:** read `references/story.md`. Decide how the protagonist can discover, test and exploit a previously established weakness. The ending must defeat the threat or contain it through a stable mechanism, then show what changed for the people afterward.
3. **Write the narration:** for an 80–90 second episode, target roughly 210–240 spoken words. Use a sincere documentary voice, one clear rule and escalating evidence. Avoid winking at the premise, disposable lore and last-second powers.
4. **Storyboard the evidence:** read `references/tension.md` and `references/visual.md`. Every beat must visibly change the situation. Show the rule, breach, warning signs, monster behavior, clue, test, plan, confrontation and aftermath. Use 10–14 materially different setups for a 90-second short.
5. **Choose the visual mode:** use original blocky toon 3D when Rey wants game-world characters, or restrained stylized 2D when silhouettes and graphic lighting teach the scene better. Keep the protagonist active and visually continuous. Never copy proprietary characters, maps, UI, sounds or branded game assets.
6. **Prepare voice and sound:** read `references/audio.md`. Preserve approved wording and natural narration speed. Use sparse atmosphere, location sound and event-timed effects; tension should not mask the voice. Deliver a clean actor brief or a separate TTS-ready block as requested.
7. **Build and review:** reuse sibling `reyxplainer-studio` for Remotion timing, captions, audio, render and QA. Use sibling `inkbeat-shorts` for general character, acting and camera systems when appropriate. Review targeted stills, the contact sheet, the full render and the mix before claiming success.

## Story invariants

- Open with the rule or immediate consequence. “In this town…” is the default hook when requested, not a requirement for unrelated horror formats.
- The world adapts sensibly to the threat. Town rules, infrastructure and behavior should reflect its history.
- Establish the monster's mechanism through at least two observable clues before the climax.
- The protagonist must notice, test and act on the solution. An unintroduced rescuer, random weapon, dream reveal or accidental victory is not a resolution.
- The climax must use the established mechanism. A strange premise does not justify arbitrary physics.
- Resolve the central problem. The monster may be destroyed, deprived of what sustains it, or securely contained with a maintained system. Do not merely put it back to sleep unless that is an explicitly chosen temporary ending.
- End with aftermath: show the new rule, repaired system, changed behavior or cost of survival. Do not undercut the payoff with an unrelated gag.
- Avoid graphic gore by default. Build fear through anticipation, scale, sound, negative space, consequences and character decisions.
